# Concept: Structural Discovery in FacetedFS
## A new way to explore and understand information collections

# The central idea
Today's information systems organize information primarily around storage locations, folders, projects and administrative structures.

These structures serve operational purposes, but they limit how we can explore and understand the information.

A document collection contains many possible relationships and patterns. Most remain invisible because we only navigate it through one dominant hierarchy.

The idea is to dynamically create alternative hierarchical views of the same information collection, allowing people to discover patterns and relationships that become visible only through a particular arrangement.

The underlying documents never move. Their classification and hierarchical presentation change.

# How it works conceptually

Documents receive structured classification values, increasingly with AI-assisted classification.
These values can be combined into different hierarchical structures.
Users can switch between hierarchies or examine several simultaneously.
Each hierarchy exposes different relationships and patterns.
Users discover information that was already present but previously difficult or impossible to perceive.

**Example: A photo collection**
The same photographs can be explored through different hierarchies:

## Hierarchy

What becomes visible:
- Year → Location
  - Your life and travels over time
- Location → Year
  - How places changed over decades
- Person → Location → Year
    -How someone's life and relationships evolved
- Event → Person → Location
  - Connections between people and experiences

The photographs haven't changed. The *information you can discover* has.

**Example: Enterprise documentation**
A repository can be explored through:

Project → Date → Department

Date → Department → Project

Author → Department → Project

System → Component → Document type

Comparing these views can reveal changing authorship, departmental involvement, technical development and relationships between documents that were previously separated by organizational filing structures.

# The problem
Three fundamental limitations of today's information systems:

Storage hierarchy dominates information hierarchy. Where something is stored determines how people encounter it.

Search replaces understanding the collection. Users see a small, constantly changing window instead of the complete information space and its distributions.

Information is treated as a collection of individual items. The patterns that emerge from examining entire collections in different structures remain hidden.

# Why now?
AI makes classification and reclassification economically practical.

Previously, creating and maintaining multiple classification systems was expensive and labor-intensive. AI can propose classifications, evaluate documents and help users establish new structures without manually reorganizing the underlying repository.

The innovation isn't AI search. AI makes a different way of working with information feasible.

# Working terminology
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
|**Structural discovery**|     Discovering patterns through alternative hierarchical arrangements|
|**Alternative hierarchies**|  Different organizational views of the same collection|
|**Classification dimensions**| Structured properties used to organize information|
|**Parallel views**| Simultaneous exploration through different hierarchies|
|**Latent information**| Patterns and relationships that become visible through a particular structure|

# The central thesis
**Information has more than one structure.**
We have traditionally organized information according to where it belongs. But the way we organize it determines what we are able to discover.

By dynamically changing its hierarchical structure, we can reveal relationships, patterns and insights that were previously hidden in plain sight.
